    What is measured · setup

    Received and sent bytes are the successful byte counts reported by observed kernel socket operations. Sent means accepted by the socket, not delivered to the peer. Peeks, failed operations and UDP are excluded. A receive operation can contain part of one message or many messages.

    Messages processed counts calls to an explicit application completion marker, one call per successfully processed message. It is never inferred from read calls or bytes.

    Start the optional source on the target host: build/triangulator-socket-sampler PID COLLECTOR-IP:PORT build/socket.bpf.o. Add MARKER-BINARY MARKER-SYMBOL for completion counts. The source needs kernel BTF, socket length tracepoints and BPF tracing privileges. The normal sampler continues without extra privileges. See docs/socket-ingress-design.md for coverage and setup.

    How to read this page

    States come from the thread's scheduler state and kernel wait channel (/proc/<tid>/wchan), readable without extra privileges; futex waits cannot be split into lock, condition or timed idle. CPU is per thread, relative to one core, so a process can exceed 100%. Thread-table read/write rates mix read- and write-family syscall traffic and do not cover every receive API; use the socket panel for explicitly observed socket operations.
    

    The process overview is computed in this browser tab from the live feed and kept for up to 15 minutes (it survives a reload of this tab, not a new tab). Per-thread history beyond that comes from the collector's 5-second summaries in SQLite. The assessment uses fixed rules of thumb: a thread at or above 90% of a core, run delay over a quarter of CPU time, any thread in uninterruptible kernel wait, any major page fault, stopped threads, sampler silence and packet loss over 1%. Treat it as a pointer to where to look, not a diagnosis.
    

    Pressure, limits and sockets come from the sampler's resource samples (every resource_interval_s, 5 s by default), read without privileges: pressure stall information for the host and the target's cgroup, /proc/<pid>/fd, limits and io, the target's own sockets through sock_diag (what ss uses), and its network namespace's counters. Their assessment rules: I/O pressure over 10% (some) or 5% (full); memory pressure over 5% or 2%; CPU pressure over 20%; descriptors over 75% of the soft limit; a buffer or accept backlog over 80% full; any accept-queue, receive-buffer or UDP drops; retransmissions over 1% of segments; TCP memory pressure; and 10 or more connections in CLOSE-WAIT. See docs/resource-monitoring.md.
